## Compounding Half-Yearly, Quarterly & Applications to Population Growth and Depreciation

CLASS 8 MATHS · CHAPTER 5 (COMPARING QUANTITIES USING PROPORTION) · STORYMODE 07

**LLM KEY / FUNCTION NAME:** `compounding_periods_half_yearly_quarterly_depreciation`
**CHAPTER:** Class 8 · Chapter 5 — Comparing Quantities using Proportion
**TEXTBOOK THREAD:** the conversion period, half-yearly and quarterly
compounding, population growth and depreciation as one repeated-percentage
structure.

## Learning objective

By the end of the video a student can: define the **conversion period**; change
the rate **and** the period count together (`R → R/2`, `n → 2n`; `R → R/4`,
`n → 4n`); compute a half-yearly or quarterly amount; use `P(1 + r)ⁿ` for
population growth and `P(1 − r)ⁿ` for depreciation; count **periods**, not years
(1½ years half-yearly is 3 periods); and apply the five-step decision map.

**Primary takeaway:**
**period → rate → factor → repeat** ·
growth `A = P(1 + R/100)ⁿ` · depreciation `V = P(1 − R/100)ⁿ`

## Pedagogical safeguards honoured

- **Rate and period count change together** (s2, stated and shown on two chips at
  once), and "10% per annum compounded half-yearly" is 5% per half-year — the
  "10% every six months" card is struck through.
- **Periods are counted, not years** (1½ years half-yearly is 3 periods).
- **The quarterly formula is labelled an extension** of the conversion-period
  rule, not a quotation.
- **Population growth re-bases** on the new total (6250 → 6750 → 7290).
- **Depreciation takes the next reduction from the remaining value**
  (100% → 90% → 81%, written as 0.9 twice), with the bouncing-ball example and
  the exercise's definition of depreciation.
- **Growth and depreciation differ only in the sign of the factor**, and the two
  decisions ("how often" vs "up or down") stay separate.
- **The new summary piece** repeats all of this in one place, so the lesson ends
  with the three cases, both formulas and the five steps together.
