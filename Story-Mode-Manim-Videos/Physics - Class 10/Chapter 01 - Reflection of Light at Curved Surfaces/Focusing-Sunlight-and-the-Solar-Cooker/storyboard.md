# Production-Ready Storyboard

## Focusing Sunlight & the Solar Cooker: How a Concave Mirror Concentrates Solar Energy

**CLASS 10 PHYSICS · TG BOARD · CHAPTER 01 — REFLECTION OF LIGHT AT CURVED SURFACES · STORYMODE 08**

**LLM KEY / FUNCTION NAME:** `focusing_sunlight_and_solar_cooker`
**DIRECT TOPIC:** Show parallel sunlight striking a concave reflector, reflected rays converging at the focus \(F\), and a vessel positioned at the focal region to receive concentrated sunlight
**RECOMMENDED VIDEO TITLE:** *How a Solar Cooker Uses a Concave Mirror to Focus Sunlight*
**TARGET CLASS LEVEL:** Class 10
**ESTIMATED DURATION:** ~4 minutes 10 seconds

Directly supported by **Section 1.7 — Making of Solar Cooker** in the Telangana textbook: a concave mirror focuses parallel sun rays at its focal point; the cooker faces the Sun and the vessel is placed at the focal point, where it becomes hot enough to cook rice.

---

# 1. Learning Objective

Students should be able to:

- explain why sunlight reaching a mirror can be treated as approximately parallel;
- recall that a concave mirror reflects rays parallel to the principal axis toward its principal focus \(F\);
- identify pole \(P\), principal axis, focus \(F\) and the concave reflecting surface;
- tell sunlight spread over a wide area from sunlight concentrated near the focus;
- explain why the focal region becomes hotter and why the vessel goes at the focal region;
- explain why orientation toward the Sun matters;
- connect a textbook ray rule to a practical device.

### Primary takeaway

# **PARALLEL SUN RAYS + CONCAVE MIRROR → CONVERGENCE AT \(F\)**

and therefore \(\boxed{\text{Place the vessel near the focal region}}\).

---

# 2. Central Story Hook — “Sunlight Feels Warm. Can a Mirror Make It Much Hotter?”

A cooking vessel sits in ordinary sunlight; wide sunlight beams cover ground, vessel and surroundings.

# **“SUNLIGHT IS ALREADY FALLING ON THE VESSEL. WHY USE A MIRROR?”**

A large concave reflector appears; several rays strike its wide surface and head, after reflection, toward one small region. The vessel moves there; its temperature indicator rises.

# **“WHAT CHANGED?”**

# **THE SAME MIRROR COLLECTED LIGHT FROM A LARGE AREA AND DIRECTED IT TOWARD A SMALL REGION.**

---

# 3. Core Visual Metaphor — “The Sunlight Funnel”

One persistent set of parallel rays.

`WIDE COLLECTION AREA` ⇓ `CONCAVE REFLECTION` ⇓ `SMALL FOCAL REGION` ⇓ `STRONG HEATING`

A glowing focal marker \(\boxed{F}\), then the vessel at this bright focal region. "Energy concentration" is a pedagogical explanation of the textbook's heating effect.

---

# 4. Core Textbook Concepts

- **Sun gives nearly parallel rays:** rays from a sufficiently distant source are nearly parallel; the Sun is an intense, distant source.
- **Concave mirror and focus:** moving a small paper to the **smallest, brightest spot** (the Sun's image); Sun rays parallel to the principal axis converge at the focus \(F\).
- **Focal length:** \(f=PF\).
- **Solar cooker:** a large concave arrangement heats a vessel placed at the focal point, with the cooker facing the Sun.

# 6. Detailed Storyboard

## SCENE 1 — WHY NOT JUST PUT THE VESSEL IN SUNLIGHT?
Sun above, vessel below, five broad rays fall around it. `SUNLIGHT SPREAD OVER A LARGE AREA`; a small temperature meter beside the vessel rises only gradually. A concave reflector appears: **“WHAT CAN THIS MIRROR CHANGE?”** Incoming rays strike it; reflected rays begin converging (final focus not yet revealed).

## SCENE 2 — WHY ARE SUN RAYS DRAWN PARALLEL?
A small nearby lamp, two rays with a visible angle. Move the source farther: the angle decreases; much farther: across the mirror's width the rays are nearly parallel. Replace the lamp with a Sun icon far off-screen: `APPROXIMATELY PARALLEL` — **“SO THE MIRROR RECEIVES A BUNDLE OF PARALLEL SUN RAYS.”**

## SCENE 3 — WHAT DOES A CONCAVE MIRROR DO TO THEM?
Principal axis, concave mirror, labels \(P, F, C\). Three rays parallel to the axis strike different points; normals appear faintly for one ray (law of reflection). Reflected rays turn inward and cross at one point, which glows: \(\boxed{F}\), `PRINCIPAL FOCUS`. Memory rule: **PARALLEL TO AXIS → REFLECT THROUGH \(F\)**.

## SCENE 4 — FIND THE FOCUS: SMALLEST, BRIGHTEST SPOT
Small screen starts too close to the mirror: large, blurry patch, `NOT FOCUS`. Move it gradually: smaller and brighter. At one position: \(\boxed{\text{SMALLEST}}\) and \(\boxed{\text{BRIGHTEST}}\); drop the label \(\boxed{F}\) and show \(PF=f\).
*Safety note:* animate this; concentrated sunlight can ignite materials.

## SCENE 5 — WHY DOES THE FOCAL REGION GET HOT?
Five rays, each with a simple energy marker (no numbers). After reflection all markers arrive in a much smaller region. Compare `BEFORE: light spread over wide area` with `AT FOCUS: many reflected rays arrive in small region`. Temperature meter rises near \(F\). **“MORE OF THE COLLECTED SUNLIGHT REACHES A SMALLER REGION.”** → `STRONGER HEATING`.
*Safeguard:* do not say the mirror “creates” heat or energy; it redirects incoming solar radiation.

## SCENE 6 — TURN THE RAY RULE INTO A SOLAR COOKER
Large concave dish, parallel sunlight, \(P\) and \(F\) marked. Rays strike the reflector; reflected rays converge near \(F\), which glows. The cooking vessel enters from outside the frame and moves slowly along the axis; its centre aligns with \(F\): \(\boxed{\text{VESSEL AT }F}\). The temperature meter rises, steam appears subtly: `CONCENTRATED SUNLIGHT HEATS THE VESSEL`.

## SCENE 7 — WHY MUST THE COOKER FACE THE SUN?
Aligned cooker: rays converge tightly, `GOOD ALIGNMENT`. Rotate the cooker away: incoming rays are no longer parallel to its axis, the bright region shifts, the heat meter falls: `VESSEL MISSES THE CONCENTRATED REGION`. Rotate back: focus returns onto the vessel. **POINT THE REFLECTOR TOWARD THE SUN.**

## SCENE 8 — THE VESSEL CANNOT BE PLACED JUST ANYWHERE
Three ghost vessel positions: **A** before the focus, **B** at the focus, **C** beyond the focus. Before \(F\): rays still converging, spread over a larger part of the vessel, heat moderate. At \(F\): tightest bundle, heat highest. Beyond \(F\): rays have crossed and spread again, large spot, heat lower. `BEFORE F → SPREAD` · `AT F → MOST CONCENTRATED` · `AFTER F → SPREAD AGAIN`.
*Source note:* the textbook directly instructs placing the vessel at the focal point; the three-position heating comparison is a model-created visualization.

## SCENE 9 — YOUR TURN
- **A** Three rays parallel to the axis strike a concave mirror: where do the reflected rays go? → \(\boxed{F}\).
- **B** Three locations \(A, F, B\): where should the vessel go? → \(\boxed{F}\).
- **C** Plane, convex or concave? → \(\boxed{\text{CONCAVE}}\) (it converges parallel Sun rays).
- **D** What if the vessel is far beyond \(F\)? → `LIGHT IS MORE SPREAD OUT THERE · MOVE THE VESSEL TOWARD F`.

## SCENE 10 — FINALIZATION / RECAP
Sun far left, concave cooker at right. Step 1 `NEARLY PARALLEL SUNLIGHT`; step 2 `REFLECTION`; step 3 `FOCAL POINT`; step 4 \(\boxed{\text{VESSEL AT FOCAL REGION}}\); step 5 `CONCENTRATED SOLAR ENERGY → HEATING`. Final flow: PARALLEL SUN RAYS ⇓ CONCAVE REFLECTOR ⇓ FOCUS \(F\) ⇓ VESSEL ⇓ HEATING / COOKING.

# **COLLECT → REFLECT → FOCUS → HEAT**

---

# 7. Key Pedagogical Safeguards

- **A.** The reflector redirects sunlight; it does not produce energy.
- **B.** The Sun's rays are approximately parallel because the Sun is extremely distant relative to the mirror.
- **C.** Keep "parallel to the principal axis" visible: the rule is *rays parallel to the axis reflect through \(F\)*, not *any ray goes to \(F\)*.
- **D.** Do not confuse \(F\) and \(C\); the vessel goes at \(F\).
- **E.** Let students see the spot sharpen first, then name it \(F\).
- **F.** Keep the video on the optical principle, not cooker construction.
- **G.** Treat focused sunlight as a real hazard: prefer animation; any live demonstration is teacher-supervised, brief, outdoors, away from eyes, skin and flammable material.

# 8. Final Visual Learning Journey

SUN IS FAR AWAY ⇓ RAYS REACH THE MIRROR NEARLY PARALLEL ⇓ CONCAVE MIRROR REFLECTS THEM INWARD ⇓ THE RAYS CONVERGE AT THE FOCUS \(F\) ⇓ PUT THE VESSEL THERE ⇓ STRONG HEATING (SOLAR COOKING).

# 9. Final Manim Production Direction

The most important production element is **ray continuity**: one persistent set of incoming rays that (1) arrive parallel, (2) strike different points of the concave mirror, (3) reflect inward, (4) converge near \(F\), (5) strike the cooking vessel.

The visual climax: the vessel is first placed away from the focal point (the ray bundle misses or spreads across it), then slides slowly toward \(F\); the illuminated spot shrinks, brightness increases, the temperature meter rises; at \(\text{VESSEL CENTRE}\approx F\) it locks and all reflected rays hit its receiving surface.

Final shot: Sun → parallel rays → concave dish → \(F\) → vessel, with the closing anchor:

# **PARALLEL RAYS → FOCUS \(F\)** · **VESSEL AT \(F\) → CONCENTRATED HEATING** · **THAT IS THE OPTICAL IDEA BEHIND THE SOLAR COOKER.**
