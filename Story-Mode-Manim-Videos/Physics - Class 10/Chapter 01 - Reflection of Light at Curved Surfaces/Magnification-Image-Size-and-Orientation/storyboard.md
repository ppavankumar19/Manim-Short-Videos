# Production-Ready Storyboard

## Magnification: Connecting Image Size, Orientation, \(h_i/h_o\) and \(-v/u\)

**CLASS 10 PHYSICS · TG BOARD · CHAPTER 01 — REFLECTION OF LIGHT AT CURVED SURFACES · STORYMODE 07**

**LLM KEY / FUNCTION NAME:** `mirror_magnification_image_size_and_orientation`
**DIRECT TOPIC:** Connect
\[
m=\frac{h_i}{h_o}=-\frac{v}{u}
\]
to enlarged/diminished images and upright/inverted orientation
**RECOMMENDED VIDEO TITLE:** *Magnification in Mirrors: Image Size, Orientation & the Formula \(m=-v/u\)*
**TARGET CLASS LEVEL:** Class 10
**ESTIMATED DURATION:** ~4 minutes 15 seconds

The textbook introduces magnification immediately after the mirror formula and defines it using the ratio of image height to object height. From the geometry and sign convention, it derives

\[
\boxed{
m=\frac{h_i}{h_o}=-\frac{v}{u}
}
\]

for spherical mirrors.

The strongest storyboard approach is therefore not to begin with formula manipulation, but with a visual question:

# **“How much bigger or smaller is the image—and why is it sometimes upside down?”**

---

# 1. Learning Objective

By the end of the video, students should be able to:

- understand magnification as a comparison between image size and object size;
- identify \(h_o\) (object height) and \(h_i\) (image height);
- use \(m=\dfrac{h_i}{h_o}\);
- connect magnification to object and image distances: \(m=-\dfrac vu\);
- understand that the **magnitude** of \(m\) tells how the image size compares with the object;
- understand that the **sign** of \(m\) carries orientation information;
- distinguish enlarged, same-size and diminished images;
- distinguish upright/erect and inverted images;
- solve the textbook example where \(h_o=+4\text{ cm}\), \(u=-25\text{ cm}\), \(v=-37.5\text{ cm}\);
- obtain \(h_i=-6\text{ cm}\) and interpret the image as **inverted and enlarged**.

The textbook's worked example reaches exactly this result.

---

# 2. Core Story Hook — One Object, Three Possible Image Sizes

### INITIAL FRAME

One upright candle.

Beside it, three image silhouettes:

1. taller than the candle;
2. same height;
3. shorter than the candle.

Question:

# **“CAN ONE NUMBER TELL US ALL THREE?”**

Then reveal \(m\) and underneath `MAGNIFICATION`.

Now flip one image upside down.

Ask:

# **“CAN THE SAME NUMBER ALSO TELL US WHETHER THE IMAGE IS UPRIGHT OR INVERTED?”**

Then reveal \(\boxed{m=\frac{h_i}{h_o}}\).

The video becomes a decoding exercise:

# **MAGNIFICATION = SIZE + ORIENTATION INFORMATION**

---

# 3. Core Visual Metaphor

## “The Magnification Meter”

Keep a vertical object-height bar on the left (\(h_o\)) and a dynamic image-height bar on the right (\(h_i\)). Between them, show a live magnification meter \(m=\frac{h_i}{h_o}\).

### Meter states

- \(|m|>1\): `ENLARGED`
- \(|m|=1\): `SAME SIZE`
- \(|m|<1\): `DIMINISHED`

Then use the sign as an orientation switch:

- \(m>0\) → same vertical direction as object.
- \(m<0\) → opposite vertical direction.

This sign interpretation is a direct mathematical reading of the textbook's signed height convention and magnification formula, rather than a separately stated textbook rule. The source states that heights measured upward from the principal axis are positive and downward are negative.

---

# 4. Core Textbook Content

## Definition of Magnification

\[
\boxed{m=\frac{\text{height of image}}{\text{height of object}}}
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
\boxed{m=\frac{h_i}{h_o}}
\]

and, for mirrors, \(\boxed{m=-\dfrac vu}\).

## Height Sign Convention

- object/image height measured **upward** from the principal axis → positive;
- height measured **downward** from the principal axis → negative.

Therefore, for an upright object \(h_o>0\). If the image is inverted, \(h_i<0\).

# 6. Detailed Storyboard

## SCENE 1 — WHAT DOES MAGNIFICATION ACTUALLY TELL US?

**TIME:** 00:00–00:20 · **PURPOSE:** Make magnification feel useful before presenting formulas.

One upright candle (object, 4 cm).

- **0–5 sec** Image 1 appears taller. `ENLARGED`
- **5–9 sec** Transform to same-height image. `SAME SIZE`
- **9–13 sec** Transform to smaller image. `DIMINISHED`
- **13–17 sec** Image flips below the principal axis. `INVERTED`
- **17–20 sec** Question: **“CAN ONE NUMBER DESCRIBE BOTH SIZE AND ORIENTATION?”** Reveal \(\boxed{m}\).

Transition: the candle and image move onto a principal axis.

---

## SCENE 2 — HEIGHTS HAVE SIGNS TOO

**TIME:** 00:20–00:45 · **PURPOSE:** Prepare students to interpret the sign of magnification.

- **0–6 sec** Vertical arrow grows upward from the axis: \(h_o\), \(\boxed{h_o>0}\).
- **6–12 sec** Upright image appears, \(\boxed{h_i>0}\).
- **12–18 sec** Image transforms below the axis, arrow runs downward, \(\boxed{h_i<0}\).
- **18–24 sec** Memory card: `UPWARD HEIGHT → +`, `DOWNWARD HEIGHT → −`.

---

## SCENE 3 — BUILD MAGNIFICATION AS A SIZE RATIO

**TIME:** 00:45–01:07 · **PURPOSE:** Make the definition intuitive.

Object height \(h_o=4\text{ cm}\); image height \(h_i=8\text{ cm}\).

- **0–5 sec** \(m=\dfrac{\text{image height}}{\text{object height}}\).
- **5–10 sec** \(\boxed{m=\dfrac{h_i}{h_o}}\).
- **10–15 sec** Magnitudes only, for size intuition: \(\dfrac84=2\). The image bar is visibly twice as tall.
- **15–20 sec** `IMAGE IS 2 TIMES THE OBJECT'S HEIGHT`.

Animate the numerator \(h_i\) physically moving over \(h_o\).

---

## SCENE 4 — THE MAGNITUDE OF \(m\) TELLS THE SIZE

**TIME:** 01:07–01:31 · **PURPOSE:** Build the enlarged / same / diminished classification.

Magnification scale \(0\cdots1\cdots2\).

- **0–7 sec** Needle to \(|m|=2\); image doubles. \(|m|>1\): `ENLARGED`.
- **7–14 sec** Needle to \(|m|=1\); heights coincide. \(|m|=1\): `SAME SIZE` (the centre-of-curvature case).
- **14–21 sec** Needle to \(|m|=0.5\); image shrinks. \(|m|<1\): `DIMINISHED`.

On-screen rule: **IGNORE THE SIGN FOR A MOMENT → \(|m|\) TELLS SIZE**.

---

## SCENE 5 — THE SIGN OF \(m\) TELLS ORIENTATION

**TIME:** 01:31–01:53 · **PURPOSE:** Connect signed height to upright/inverted orientation.

- **0–6 sec** Positive case: upright image, \(m=\frac{+h_i}{+h_o}>0\), `SAME ORIENTATION / ERECT`.
- **6–13 sec** Negative case: image flips downward, \(m=\frac{-h_i}{+h_o}<0\), `INVERTED`.

Final memory card: **\(|m|\) → SIZE · SIGN OF \(m\) → ORIENTATION**.

---

## SCENE 6 — WHY CAN WE ALSO WRITE \(m=-v/u\)?

**TIME:** 01:53–02:17 · **PURPOSE:** Connect image size to object/image positions.

Mirror, object and image with \(h_o,h_i,u,v\) marked.

- **0–6 sec** Highlight the triangles used in the magnification derivation.
- **6–12 sec** \(\dfrac{h_i}{h_o}\) morphs into \(-\dfrac vu\).
- **12–18 sec** \(\boxed{m=\dfrac{h_i}{h_o}=-\dfrac vu}\).
- **18–24 sec** `SIZE INFORMATION CAN COME FROM HEIGHTS OR DISTANCES`.

Production note: do not reproduce the derivation line by line; this video is about meaning and application.

---

## SCENE 7 — TEXTBOOK EXAMPLE: FIND THE IMAGE HEIGHT

**TIME:** 02:17–02:50 · **PURPOSE:** Combine the previous mirror-formula result with magnification.

Data: \(h_o=+4\text{ cm}\), \(u=-25\text{ cm}\), \(v=-37.5\text{ cm}\).

- **0–6 sec** \(\dfrac{h_i}{h_o}=-\dfrac vu\).
- **6–12 sec** \(\dfrac{h_i}{4}=-\dfrac{-37.5}{-25}\).
- **12–18 sec** \(-\dfrac{-37.5}{-25}=-\dfrac{37.5}{25}\).
- **18–22 sec** \(=-1.5\), so \(m=-1.5\).
- **22–27 sec** \(\dfrac{h_i}{4}=-1.5\), \(h_i=-6\text{ cm}\).
- **27–33 sec** \(\boxed{h_i=-6\text{ cm}}\).

---

## SCENE 8 — READ THE ANSWER PHYSICALLY

**TIME:** 02:50–03:13 · **PURPOSE:** Stop students treating \(-6\) cm as "just a negative number."

- **0–6 sec** \(|-6|=6\) against \(h_o=4\): \(6>4\) → `ENLARGED`.
- **6–12 sec** Isolate the minus: the image arrow flips below the axis → `INVERTED`.
- **12–18 sec** \(m=-1.5\): \(|m|=1.5>1\) → enlarged; \(m<0\) → inverted.

On-screen text: **\(-1.5\) DOES NOT MEAN “SMALLER.” THE SIZE COMES FROM \(|m|=1.5\).**

---

## SCENE 9 — YOUR TURN: DECODE \(m\)

**TIME:** 03:13–03:45

- **A** \(m=+2\): `ENLARGED`, `ERECT`.
- **B** \(m=-0.5\): `DIMINISHED`, `INVERTED`.
- **C** \(m=-1\): `SAME SIZE`, `INVERTED` (the centre-of-curvature case).
- **D** \(u=-20\text{ cm},\ v=-40\text{ cm}\): \(m=-\dfrac{-40}{-20}=-2\), `2× ENLARGED + INVERTED`.

---

## SCENE 10 — FINALIZATION / RECAP

**TIME:** 03:45–04:15

Central: \(\boxed{m=\dfrac{h_i}{h_o}=-\dfrac vu}\).

- **Question 1 — HOW BIG?** Look only at \(|m|\): \(>1\) enlarged, \(=1\) same size, \(<1\) diminished.
- **Question 2 — WHICH WAY?** \(m>0\) erect, \(m<0\) inverted.
- **Textbook example:** \(m=-1.5\): enlarged, inverted.
- **Final decoder:** magnitude of \(m\) → size; sign of \(m\) → orientation.

### FINAL MESSAGE

# **DON'T JUST CALCULATE \(m\). READ WHAT \(m\) IS TELLING YOU.**

---

# 7. Key Pedagogical Safeguards

- **A.** Magnification is a ratio with no unit; \(h_i,h_o\) are lengths.
- **B.** Use \(|m|\) to judge enlarged/diminished. \(m=-2\) is **not** "diminished": \(|m|=2>1\) is enlarged; the minus sign tells orientation only.
- **C.** Connect the sign back to the height convention (upward \(+\), downward \(-\)); derive orientation, do not memorise it.
- **D.** Keep \(m=h_i/h_o\) and \(m=-v/u\) visually connected as one relationship.
- **E.** Preserve signed \(u\) and \(v\): \(u=-25,\ v=-37.5\) give \(m=-1.5\).
- **F.** Interpret every result: never end at \(m=-1.5\); translate it to enlarged + inverted.

---

# 8. Final Visual Learning Journey

1. Calculate magnification: \(m=\frac{h_i}{h_o}\) or \(m=-\frac vu\).
2. Ignore the sign: \(|m|>1\) enlarged, \(|m|=1\) same size, \(|m|<1\) diminished.
3. Look at the sign: \(m>0\) erect, \(m<0\) inverted.

Textbook example: \(m=-\dfrac{-37.5}{-25}=-1.5\) → enlarged and inverted; \(h_i=mh_o=(-1.5)(4)=-6\text{ cm}\).

---

# 9. Final Manim Production Direction

The defining animation device is the **Magnification Meter**: object-height arrow, image-height arrow and a magnification readout. When the image grows, update \(|m|\) live (0.5 → 1 → 1.5 → 2), passing through `DIMINISHED`, `SAME SIZE`, `ENLARGED`. Then, without changing size, flip the image across the axis and transform the readout \(+1.5\to-1.5\): **size did not change, orientation changed, because only the sign changed.**

For the textbook example, keep continuity with the previous video: \(u=-25\text{ cm}\), \(v=-37.5\text{ cm}\) already visible, transformed directly into \(m=-\frac vu\). Finish by splitting \(m=-1.5\) into \(\boxed{1.5}\to\text{ENLARGED}\) and \(\boxed{-}\to\text{INVERTED}\).

The conceptual journey: **Object Height → Image Height → Magnification Ratio → Distance Formula → Magnitude Gives Size → Sign Gives Orientation.**
