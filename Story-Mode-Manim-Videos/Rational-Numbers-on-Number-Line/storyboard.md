# Production-Ready Storyboard — Representation of Rational Numbers on a Number Line

The storyboard below follows the Class 8 textbook treatment: rational numbers are written in the form \(p/q\), where \(p,q\) are integers and \(q\neq0\); for number-line representation, the **denominator determines the number of equal parts in each unit interval**, while the **numerator determines how many of those parts are counted**. The textbook specifically illustrates numbers such as \(-\frac12,\frac32\) and the example \(\frac37,\frac57\).

---

## VIDEO OVERVIEW

**VIDEO TITLE:**
**Where Do Fractions Live? — Rational Numbers on a Number Line**

**TOPIC:**
Representation of Rational Numbers on a Number Line

**TARGET CLASS LEVEL:**
Classes 6–12, with primary focus on Class 8

**ESTIMATED DURATION:**
Approximately **2 minutes 35 seconds**

**LEARNING OBJECTIVE:**
By the end of the video, students should be able to locate a positive or negative rational number on a number line by:

1. Identifying the sign and direction.
2. Dividing each unit interval into equal parts according to the denominator.
3. Counting the required number of parts according to the numerator.
4. Correctly locating the rational number.

**HISTORICAL HOOK:**
People needed fractions to describe quantities that were not whole numbers—for example, portions of measured lengths, weights, land, and goods. A number line provides a visual way to place these quantities in their exact order and position.

**CORE VISUAL METAPHOR / IDEA:**

> **Denominator = How many equal pieces?**
> **Numerator = How many pieces do we move?**

The number line becomes a "measuring ruler" that is repeatedly divided into equal pieces.

**FORMULA(S):**

$$
\boxed{\frac{p}{q}},\qquad q\neq0
$$

For representation:

* \(|q|\) → number of equal parts in one unit interval
* \(|p|\) → number of parts counted from \(0\)
* Positive sign → move right
* Negative sign → move left

**WORKED EXAMPLE:**

$$
\boxed{\frac37}
$$

Divide \(0\) to \(1\) into **7 equal parts**, then count **3 parts from 0 toward 1**.

**REAL-WORLD APPLICATION:**
A measuring ruler/tape where a length such as \(\frac34\) metre needs to be located precisely between \(0\) and \(1\) metre.

**STUDENT CHALLENGE:**

$$
\boxed{-\frac45}
$$

Ask students to locate it on a number line before revealing the answer.

## SCENE 1 — WHY DO WE NEED NUMBERS BETWEEN WHOLE NUMBERS?

**TIME:**
**00:00–00:16**

### PURPOSE

Create the need for fractions before introducing the formal definition.

### INITIAL FRAME

A simple historical/practical measurement scene:

* A horizontal measuring rope/ruler.
* One complete unit marked.
* A length that is clearly **less than 1 whole unit**.
* The measurement cannot be described accurately using only whole numbers.

Avoid detailed historical characters. The focus should be the mathematical problem.

### VISUAL LAYOUT

**Top:**
Small contextual heading:

`MEASURING A LENGTH`

**Center:**
Horizontal measuring strip.

**Bottom:**
Whole-number scale:

`0 ───────────── 1`

A point appears between 0 and 1.

### VISUAL ELEMENTS

* Measurement strip
* Tick marks
* 0 and 1
* Fractional point
* Arrow pointing to the point
* \(\frac12\), \(\frac34\) appearing briefly

### ANIMATION SEQUENCE

**0–3 sec:**
Measurement strip draws from left to right.

**3–6 sec:**
Mark `0` and `1`.

**6–9 sec:**
A measured point appears between them.

**9–12 sec:**
The space between 0 and 1 visually divides into equal sections.

**12–16 sec:**
\(\frac12\) and \(\frac34\) briefly appear over fractional positions.

### ON-SCREEN TEXT

`What about the numbers in between?`

### MATHEMATICAL ELEMENTS

$$
0 \qquad \frac12 \qquad \frac34 \qquad 1
$$

### NARRATION INTENT

Explain that when measuring lengths, quantities are often not exactly 0, 1, 2, etc. We need numbers that describe **parts of a whole**.

Do not give the formal rational-number definition yet.

### MANIM IMPLEMENTATION IDEA

* `NumberLine`
* `Line`
* `VGroup`
* `Create()`
* `FadeIn()`
* `GrowFromPoint()`
* `Indicate()`

The measurement strip can later transform directly into the number line.

### CAMERA / FOCUS

Keep the measurement point near the center. Avoid zooming into tiny tick marks.

### TRANSITION TO NEXT SCENE

The measurement strip **stretches horizontally and becomes a number line** extending from \(-2\) to \(2\).

The video should feel like **one continuous mathematical construction**, rather than eight unrelated slides.


# MATHEMATICAL ACCURACY CHECK

The underlying textbook states that rational numbers are numbers expressible as

$$
\frac pq,\qquad p,q\in\mathbb Z,\quad q\neq0
$$

and specifically explains that, when representing rational numbers on a number line, the **denominator indicates how many equal parts each unit is divided into**, while the **numerator indicates how many of those parts are considered**.

The textbook's worked example identifies points \(A\) and \(B\) after dividing \(0\) to \(1\) into **7 equal parts**, with \(A=\frac37\) and \(B=\frac57\).

The accompanying exercise also explicitly asks students to represent \(\frac97\) and \(-\frac75\), reinforcing that the same number-line method applies to positive and negative rational numbers.
