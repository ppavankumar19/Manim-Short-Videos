# PYTHAGOREAN THEOREM: History + ADEPT Method
### Manim Animation Storyboard

**Goal:** Build a concept-first animation that moves from the historical need for right-triangle measurement into ADEPT: Analogy, Diagram, Example, Plain-English explanation, and Technical definition, followed by misconception checking, application, and student practice.

---

### Visual Standards
* **Color Convention**:
  * $a = \text{Blue}$ (`#60A5FA` / `CHALK_BLUE`)
  * $b = \text{Green}$ (`#4ADE80` / `CHALK_GREEN`)
  * $c = \text{Orange / Amber}$ (`#FB923C` / `CHALK_ORANGE`)
* $c$ always represents the hypotenuse and is **always opposite the $90^\circ$ angle**.
* Reuse the **3-4-5 triangle** across history, analogy, diagram, and worked example.
* Keep equations and labels large enough for mobile viewing.
* Use short on-screen text; narration carries the explanation.

---

### Scene-by-Scene Breakdown

| Scene | Title | Duration | Visual / Animation | Narration |
|:---:|:---|:---:|:---|:---|
| **1** | **The Original Problem** | 7s | Ancient-style rectangular field/building plan. Highlight two perpendicular boundaries. Dashed diagonal labeled `?`. | *Thousands of years ago, people needed to measure land, construct buildings, and create accurate right angles. But how could they find a distance that was difficult to measure directly?* |
| **2** | **Ancient 3-4-5 Pattern** | 7s | Transform field into a right triangle. Label sides 3, 4, 5. Add right-angle marker and 12-knot rope segments. | *Ancient builders and surveyors discovered useful patterns. A triangle with sides 3, 4, and 5 creates a right angle.* |
| **3** | **From Pattern to Mathematics** | 6s | Build squares on each side (area 9, 16, 25). Animate $9 + 16 = 25$. | *Mathematicians realized that this was not just a useful trick. There was a deeper relationship between the sides of a right triangle.* |
| **4** | **Historical Naming** | 6s | Fade triangle slightly. Greek column/scroll motif. Reveal "Pythagorean Theorem" and $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$. | *The relationship was known before Pythagoras, but it became strongly associated with Pythagoras and the Greek tradition of mathematical proof.* |
| **5** | **A - Analogy** | 9s | Return to 3-4-5. $3 \times 3$ square with 9 unit tiles, $4 \times 4$ with 16 unit tiles, empty $5 \times 5$ square outline on hypotenuse. | *Imagine these squares as floor tiles. The first contains 9 tiles and the second 16. Together, they contain exactly 25 tiles.* |
| **6** | **Analogy Visual Payoff** | 8s | Animate the 9 blue tiles and 16 green tiles moving across to fill the 25-cell hypotenuse square exactly. Show $9 + 16 = 25$. | *If we combine the areas of the two smaller squares, they exactly fill the square on the longest side.* |
| **7** | **D - Diagram** | 7s | Keep triangle and squares. Label legs $a$ and $b$, hypotenuse $c$. Highlight $90^\circ$, then $c$. | *In a right triangle, the two shorter sides are a and b. The side opposite the 90-degree angle is the hypotenuse, c.* |
| **8** | **Diagram Becomes Equation** | 6s | Highlight square $a \to a^2$, square $b \to b^2$, square $c \to c^2$. Transform to $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$. | *The areas of the two smaller squares add up to the area of the square on the hypotenuse.* |
| **9** | **E - Example** | 8s | Triangle with $a=3, b=4, c=?$. Animate $3^2 + 4^2 = c^2 \to 9 + 16 = c^2 \to 25 = c^2 \to c = 5$. | *For a triangle with sides 3 and 4, three squared plus four squared equals twenty-five. So the hypotenuse is five.* |
| **10** | **P - Plain English** | 6s | Simplify screen to three squares: "Small square + Small square = Big square", morph back to $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$. | *In simple words: the square on one short side plus the square on the other short side equals the square on the longest side.* |
| **11** | **T - Technical Definition** | 7s | Clean right triangle and $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$. Add $a, b = \text{legs}$; $c = \text{hypotenuse}$. | *In a right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides.* |
| **12** | **Misconception Check** | 7s | Right triangle with checkmark ($\checkmark$) vs non-right triangle with cross ($\times$). Formula appears only under right triangle. | *One important rule: this theorem applies only to right triangles. You cannot automatically use it for every triangle.* |
| **13** | **Real-World Application** | 8s | Wall, ground, leaning ladder. Label $6\text{ m}, 8\text{ m}, ?$. Animate $6^2 + 8^2 = 100 \implies c = 10\text{ m}$. | *If a ladder reaches 8 metres up a wall and its base is 6 metres away, the theorem tells us the ladder is 10 metres long.* |
| **14** | **Student Challenge** | 9s | Right triangle with $5, 12, ?$. Display "Your Turn!" with a pause, then reveal $5^2 + 12^2 = 169 \implies c = 13$. | *Now you try. The two shorter sides are 5 and 12. What is the hypotenuse?* |
| **15** | **Final Recap** | 6s | 5-step flow: Right angle $\to$ Find $c \to$ Square $a$ & $b \to$ Add $\to$ Square root. End on $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$. | *Remember: confirm the right angle, identify the hypotenuse, square the two shorter sides, add them, and solve for the missing side.* |
